Chapter 1

What RosterFlow is, and the four steps

RosterFlow is the planning program of your bus company. It is modelled on the professional software that real transport operators use, so it plans the way they do: in four steps, each building on the one before.

The tabs at the top right of RosterFlow are these four steps, in this order. Work from left to right: a change in an earlier step flows into the later ones.

The four steps in plain words

TabWhat you decideWhy it matters
TimeplanThe timetable of a line: how long the bus takes between stops, and at what times trips leave. For example, "every 10 minutes from 07:00 to 09:00".More trips mean more passengers, but also more buses and drivers.
VehiclesTrips are chained into a block (a bus's day): the bus leaves the depot, drives trip after trip and returns at night.The number of buses on the road at the busiest moment is how many buses you must own.
DriversEach bus's day is cut into duties: pieces of work one driver can do within the rules (length, breaks, rest).The number of duties and paid hours decides how many drivers you need, and your wage bill.
RosterReal drivers are put on the duties, per day: Marieke drives duty 3 on Tuesday.This is where sickness, holidays and rest times come in.
The one idea to remember
A duty (Drivers tab) is a piece of work without a name, like "duty 3, 18:10–01:51". The roster puts a name on it for a day. You design the duties once per timetable, and fill in the names every week.

Easy words for the hard words

Trip

One journey of a bus from one end of the line to the other. Shown as a coloured arrow; the point shows the direction.

Block

One bus's day: all the trips it drives between leaving and returning to the depot. In Dutch: an omloop.

Duty

One driver's working day: sign-on, pieces of work on one or more buses, breaks, sign-off.

Relief point

A place where one driver can hand the bus to another: a terminus or the depot.
Chapter 2

Switching it on and finding your way

RosterFlow is an add-on for the Normal and Realistic modes. Without it your company uses the standard planning, where the game makes the blocks and duties for you.

  1. Open your company's settingsIn the bus company, open the settings and find Add-ons.
  2. Switch on RosterFlowThe switch says it is an advanced tool, modelled on professional software. Switch it on: the logo animation plays and the guided tour is offered.
  3. Open PlanningThe company's Planning page is now RosterFlow. You can switch the add-on off again at any time; your RosterFlow plan is kept.

The screen, top to bottom

PartWhat it does
Title barThe name of your schedule with a dot (green = saved, orange = unsaved changes); undo, redo and history; the day switcher (Mon–Fri, Saturday, Sunday); the four tabs on the right.
KPI tilesThe key figures of your planning: buses at the peak (PVR), number of duties, paid hours, deadhead (empty) kilometres and costs. After every change, a small green or red number shows the difference. Green is better, red is worse.
ToolbarSAVE DISCARD Stats, Layers, Filter, Preferences, Print, and OPTIMIZE on the right.
GanttThe big chart. Time runs from left to right; each row is a bus (Vehicles) or a duty (Drivers). On the left of each row: a checkbox, a red ! when there is an issue, the row's ID, and on hover a lock and a pin.
Status barHow much is assigned; the counts of Global, Duty and Vehicle issues (click to open the list); the zoom.
Moving around the chart
Ctrl + mouse wheel zooms in and out. Shift + mouse wheel scrolls sideways. The scroll bars at the bottom and right can be dragged too.
Everything is a draft
Whatever you change in RosterFlow is a draft until you press SAVE. Until then, the game keeps running the plan you saved last. See chapter 8.
Chapter 3 · Step 1

Timeplan: when do the buses run?

The Timeplan is a line's timetable. Pick a line, a day group and a direction, then work on two screens: running times and the timetable.

The bar at the top

Choose the line, the day group (Mon–Fri, Saturday, Sunday) and the direction: OUTBOUND or INBOUND. The return trips can have their own times. On the right are the two screens: the clock icon (running times) and the grid icon (timetable). Under the bar is the stop strip: every stop with its code, on a rail in the line's colour.

Screen A · Running times

How many minutes the bus needs between two stops. Because traffic is slower in the rush hour, the day is split into time bands (for example 06:00–09:00 peak, 09:00–15:00 off-peak).

  1. Show the running timesClick the clock icon on the right of the bar.
  2. Change a running timeClick a box with minutes in a time band, type a new number and press Enter. Every trip in that band moves with it at once.
  3. Add or remove a time band⊕ adds a band, the bin removes one. Click a band's time to type a new one.

Screen B · Timetable

Each row is one trip, with its time at every stop. On the left are fixed columns: PATTERN (which stops it serves), VEHICLE TYPE, START and HEADWAY (minutes since the trip before). On top are figures: the number of trips, trips by pattern and trips by hour.

  1. Show the timetableClick the grid icon on the right of the bar.
  2. Add trips by headwayClick the clock tool above the table. Give the first and the last departure and the minutes between buses (for example 10), then click the tick. "10,20" alternates 10 and 20 minutes.
  3. Change a single tripType a new start or headway: every later trip moves with it. A time you type over a stop turns orange, with how far it is off.
  4. Save the timetableClick Save. If it asks you to mend something first, two trips leave at the same minute: undo them and add them at other times.
  5. Refresh the blocksThe line is now marked UNSYNCED in the status bar. Click Refresh: the buses' blocks and the duties are made again for the new trips.
Tip
Start simple: one headway for the whole day. When that works, add extra trips in the rush hours. Watch the KPI tiles in Vehicles: every extra trip at the peak can mean an extra bus.

Grey boxes (D) are the depot: the bus pulls out and pulls in. Coloured arrows are trips in the line's colour, labelled line-direction. The blue line on bus 101 is a cut: another driver takes over there.

Move a trip to another bus

  1. Pick up a tripDrag a coloured chip, or double-click it. Slots appear on every bus it could go to.
  2. Read the stars and the costHold the mouse over a slot. Five stars is the cheapest choice. A blue slot breaks no rule; a red one does. The card shows the cost difference.
  3. Drop itClick a slot, or let go of the chip over it. Esc puts it back.
  4. Check the figuresThe green or red numbers on the KPI tiles show what your move did.

Chapter 5 · Step 3

Drivers: what is one driver's working day?

A bus can be on the road for 18 hours, but a driver may work about 8 to 9. So each block is cut into duties. On this tab, each row is one duty: a working day without a name yet.

Cutting a bus's day into duties

Cuts are made on the Vehicles tab, because duties are cut from the buses' days.

  1. Open the Vehicles tabFind a long bus day.
  2. Cut at a terminusHold the mouse over the gap between two trips. Where a blue line shows, a driver can take over (a terminus or the depot). Click to cut there.
  3. Look at it in DriversThere is one duty more now: the second driver's day starts where you cut.
  4. Undo a cutBack in Vehicles, click the arrows where the drivers change. The two duties become one again.

Building and opening a duty

  1. Combine workIn Drivers, drag a chip of a short duty (or double-click it) and drop it on another duty. The two become one duty with a break between them. No slot appears? The duty would get too long: take a shorter piece.
  2. Open a dutyClick a duty's ID on the left. Its panel shows sign-on, the pieces of work, the breaks and sign-off. A long unpaid break makes it a split duty.
  3. Lock the dutyClick the lock at the top of the panel. OPTIMIZE will leave it as it is.
The rules RosterFlow checks
Refused (hard rules): pieces that overlap, too little rest between pieces, a working day longer than the maximum.
Warned (soft rules): overtime, a driver changing at a different stop than where they stopped, a very long break in a split duty.
You set the limits yourself under Preferences (chapter 7).
What makes a good duty
Close to a full working day (about 8 hours of paid time), breaks at a terminus, as little time waiting as possible. Fewer, fuller duties mean fewer drivers and lower wages.
Chapter 6 · Step 4

Roster: who drives what, on which day?

Now the duties get names. The Roster tab shows the coming seven days. For each day you see the lines and tours, the duties on them, and who drives each one.

  1. Pick a dayThe day tabs at the top show the next seven days. A dot shows whether every duty is covered.
  2. Give a duty a driverClick a duty, then a driver in the panel on the right (or drag the driver onto the duty). The list puts the drivers who fit first, standby drivers ahead of the rest.
  3. Read why a driver can'tEvery driver who can't take the duty shows the reason: ill or on holiday, already worked 5 days this week, no licence or type training, driving another tour then, not enough rest after yesterday, or a working day over the limit.
  4. Use the toolsRepeat Mon–Fri or Repeat on the weekend copies this day's roster to the other days of its kind. Fill the roster gives open duties to drivers who fit. Clear the day empties the day so you can start again.
When something goes wrong today
Drivers fall ill and buses break down. On the day itself, RosterFlow's issues show what fell out. A duty can be covered by a free colleague, a standby driver, an agency driver or yourself. A broken bus can be replaced by a spare bus of the line, or a rental bus. Whatever is left can be dropped, with the contract penalty shown.

Reading the colours

You seeIt means
A duty in a driver's colourCovered: that driver drives it.
Orange outlineOpen: nobody drives it yet.
Red outlineA rule is in the way, such as a clash with other work or a driver on holiday. Hover for the reason.
"No bus"The tour has no bus today, for example because it is in the workshop.
Chapter 7

OPTIMIZE, issues and preferences

OPTIMIZE: let RosterFlow make a proposal

  1. Open Vehicles or DriversOPTIMIZE is on the toolbar of both.
  2. Click OPTIMIZERosterFlow works out the blocks and duties by itself. Locked rows stay as they are.
  3. Compare the figuresThe KPI tiles now show the proposal against your planning: green where it is better, red where it is worse.
  4. Accept it or keep yoursAccept puts the proposal in your draft; Keep mine forgets it. Then adjust by hand.
A good habit
Lock what you like, then OPTIMIZE the rest. Repeat until the figures stop getting better.

Issues: the to-do list of your planning

  1. Open the issuesClick Global, Duty Issues or Vehicle Issues in the status bar. Global issues are about the whole day, such as tours nobody is planned for or a bus in the workshop.
  2. Jump to an issueClick an issue in the list: RosterFlow shows its row. Rows with an issue also have a red ! before their ID.
  3. Fix it, or dismiss itMove a trip, cut or join a duty, or change the rules under Preferences. If it is fine as it is, tick it and dismiss it.

Preferences: the rules of your company

The Preferences window has four groups: VEHICLES, DRIVERS, DEPOTS and MISC. Use the search box to find one. Each preference is either:

Hard rule

Never broken. OPTIMIZE always keeps to it, and a hand edit that breaks it is shown as an error.

Soft rule

May be broken, at a penalty. OPTIMIZE weighs the penalty against the savings.

Finish with SAVE & CLOSE. OPTIMIZE and the issue checks follow your preferences from then on.

Chapter 8

SAVE, DISCARD and what the game does with it

RosterFlow never changes your running company by accident. Everything you do is a draft until you save it.

  1. Make a changeMove a trip or cut a duty. The dot at the top turns orange: there are unsaved changes.
  2. DISCARD itDISCARD forgets your changes and goes back to what was saved.
  3. Or SAVE itSAVE makes it the company's planning. The dot turns green.
What happens after SAVE
  • The day plan and the roster use your blocks and duties.
  • The AI buses on the fleet map drive your blocks.
  • "Close the day" pays wages and counts kilometres by your duties.
  • When you drive a company duty yourself, the company launcher starts exactly the duty you planned: line, trips, bus and depot are fixed.

Printing

Print (on the toolbar) saves the bus graph or the duty graph as a PDF: the paper the real depots hang on the wall. Printing works on a computer, not on a phone.

Chapter 9

Your first full planning in 15 minutes

One line, from nothing to a running day. Follow along in the game.

  1. Timeplan: make the timetableOpen Timeplan, pick your line and Mon–Fri, OUTBOUND. Add trips by headway: first 06:00, last 22:00, every 15 minutes. Do the same for INBOUND. Save.
  2. RefreshClick UNSYNCED · Refresh in the status bar. RosterFlow makes the blocks and duties.
  3. Vehicles: look at the busesNote the PVR tile: that is how many buses you need. Do you own that many? If not, buy them first, or take fewer trips in the rush hour.
  4. OPTIMIZEClick OPTIMIZE, compare the figures and click Accept.
  5. Drivers: check the dutiesOpen a few duties. Are they around 8 hours, with a break? Fix the issues in the status bar.
  6. SAVEPress SAVE. The dot turns green.
  7. Roster: name the driversOpen Roster, pick tomorrow and click Fill the roster. Give open duties a driver by hand, or hire more drivers. Repeat on the weekend if needed.
  8. Run the dayLet the time run. Watch the fleet map, or drive a duty yourself from the duty panel ("Drive this duty").
If it gets too much
You can always switch RosterFlow off in the company settings. The standard planning takes over, and your RosterFlow plan stays saved for later.
